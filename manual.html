1. The one idea behind it

The app shows you a word. You try to remember it. You say how that went. It works out when to show you the word again — soon if you struggled, much later if you didn't.

That's the whole system. Everything else is detail.

The reason it spaces things out is that a word you review the moment before you'd have forgotten it sticks far better than one you review while it's still fresh. Reviewing something you already know solidly is close to wasted effort. So when the app tells you a word isn't due for two months, it isn't neglecting it — it's saying you don't need it yet, and it's usually right.

2. The three buttons

After you reveal the answer, you tell the app how it went.

AgainYou didn't know it, or you got it wrong.
GoodYou got there, but it took effort.
EasyYou knew it straight away, no hesitation.

Each button shows the interval it will produce, so you can see the consequence before you commit to it.

The only way to break this

Press Easy on a word you actually half-guessed. Everything the app does rests on your grades being honest. If you're generous, it schedules words too far out, you forget them, and you'll conclude the app doesn't work. Being harsh on yourself costs you a few extra reviews. Being generous costs you the word.

The rule for words with several meanings: count it as known if you produced the first meaning plus one other. Otherwise press Again.

3. Two things that will surprise you

A new word won't leave until you press Easy

Press Good on a word you're meeting for the first time and it comes back in ten minutes — not tomorrow. If nothing else is left to review, it may come back sooner rather than leave you with an empty screen. It keeps circling back until you press Easy. So a day's batch of new words stays in front of you until you've genuinely got each one, which is what you asked for. The same applies to a word you've just failed.

This only affects words being learned or relearned. A word that's settled into a normal rhythm behaves normally — Good moves it along.

Taking too long is recorded as a lower grade

The app times the gap between seeing the word and revealing the answer. It never shows a clock and never interrupts you.

  • Under ten seconds — your grade stands.
  • Ten to thirty seconds — Easy is recorded as Good.
  • Over thirty seconds — Easy is recorded as Good, and Good is recorded as a weaker pass.

Nothing is ever recorded higher than what you pressed, and Again is never altered. When a grade does get adjusted, a brief message tells you. If you put the phone down mid-card, that card's timing is discarded rather than counted against you. You can switch the whole thing off in settings.

A word you've failed before grows more slowly than one you've always got — roughly half these figures. The app tracks that per word, without you having to do anything.

Words carried over from your audit

These do not wait to appear — they arrive through the daily new-word allowance like anything else, and they come before genuinely new words. What the audit changes is the strength a word starts with: one you marked review starts about a day strong, one you marked know well starts about three weeks strong (the head start is a setting), so the app treats it as part-learned rather than new. Words you marked as known are also held back by the Known group chip, which is off by default — switch it on when you want them in the rotation.

When you fail a word you'd learned

A word due every month, failed, drops back to about two days' worth of strength and returns in ten minutes. Getting back to a month takes several sessions. That's the real cost of a lapse, and the real argument for grading honestly the first time.

5. The front screen

  • Part chips — which parts of the book are in play. Switch parts off to hold them back. Do this before you start a part, not after: switching a part off later hides words you've already begun, and they'll be overdue when you switch it back on.
  • Known / Shaky / Unseen — which of your audit groups can supply new words. Known is off by default, so you won't be shown words you already ticked. This only affects words that haven't started yet; anything already in the rotation carries on regardless.
  • Direction — Latin to English, or English to Latin. English to Latin stays locked until a word's forward card has held steady for a fortnight, because producing a word is much harder than recognising it. That's why the reverse direction is empty at first.
  • New words today — a stepper that changes today's allowance only, without touching your normal setting.
  • Start — tells you how many words are waiting.
  • Study days — a dot for each of the last five weeks' days, and your current run. Tap Full calendar underneath to open a real month view: every day you studied shows its own new-word and review count right on the box (new in green, reviewed in amber), so a whole month's shape is visible at a glance without tapping into each day, plus month navigation.
  • Pace — how many words you've met, how many are left, the date you'd finish at your recent rate, how that lands against the start of term, and what one day off costs.
  • Where you stand — how many words are in each stage, and how often you've been right over the last month. Open Timing underneath for how quickly you've been answering.
  • Struggling words — every word that's had "Again" 5 or more times in a single study day, worst first, and everything you've set aside. Tap a word's flag right there to set it aside or bring it back, without waiting to meet it in a session — or dismiss the warning with the small × below the flag if it isn't time to decide yet. See §9, A word keeps beating me, and Drilling your set-aside words below.

The whole syllabus

Below the pace panel is an estimate of how much of the list you would get right if you were tested now, and again on the first day of term — once assuming you stop today, once assuming you keep going at your recent rate. Words you have never met count as zero, so the number starts low and stays honest. These are the scheduler's own predictions rather than measurements; the how well the model is predicting you fold is the check on them, and it needs a few hundred real reviews before it means anything.

The stats page

Tap the grid icon next to the gear, top left, for a fuller picture than the front screen has room for. It always answers for the whole list in whichever direction you have switched to at the top of the page — not just the parts currently in play, since the point is to see everything, including what you haven't started.

  • Where you stand — the same stages as the front screen's panel of that name, but as one bar: still to meet, in short-term steps, settling (under three weeks), holding (three weeks or more), and set aside, in proportion to the whole list.
  • By part — a small box for every single word, grouped by part, coloured the same way as the bar above. It fills in as words move through those stages, so you can see at a glance which parts you've made a start on and which are still untouched. Greek has no parts, so this is one block for the whole list.

Browsing the full list

"Browse the full list" under The whole syllabus on the front screen opens every word in the deck at once, in either direction, grouped by part and alphabetised within each part. A search box narrows it to whatever you type — matched against the word you'd be shown, not the answer, so searching in Latin to English searches Latin terms; switch direction to search by English instead. The row filters underneath narrow by how well you know each word, the same stages as everywhere else in the app. Each part starts collapsed; tap its heading to open it, and tap any word to reveal its answer in place — this never counts as a review and never touches your scheduling, it's just a look-up. The little dot on each row is the same colour language as the stats page's boxes.

Drilling your set-aside words

"Drill these words" under the Set aside list on the front screen opens a multiple-choice pass over exactly the words you've set aside — nothing else. Every time you start it, the order is reshuffled, and every card's wrong answers are freshly chosen too, so there's nothing fixed you could learn by position instead of by meaning. Two words that just look alike — close spelling, not merely both being hard — are deliberately placed next to each other and offered as each other's wrong answer, so you drill the actual distinction between them rather than a vague feeling that both are difficult. None of this touches your real scheduling, so answer it right, answer it wrong, drill the same list again a minute later — it costs nothing. A "Bring this word back to normal review" button lets you graduate a word the moment it's stopped tripping you up, the same action as the flag, from a different screen.

6. A sensible daily routine

Open it once a day and clear what's due. That's it. The app is designed around the assumption that you show up daily; if you skip days the work doesn't vanish, it piles up.

  • Do the reviews before the new words. The app already orders it that way. If a day is going to be short, doing reviews and no new words is far better than the reverse.
  • If you fall behind, don't try to clear everything at once. Set new words to 0 for a few days and just work through the backlog.
  • Watch the "right but slow" count in the Timing panel. Those are the words about to fail, before they fail.
  • Remember the tail. New words you add today generate reviews for months. Adding 50 a day through September means carrying a few hundred reviews a day through Michaelmas. If you want term to be lighter, ease off the new words in September rather than after term starts.

Tap targets

The buttons are bigger than they look — the invisible tappable area extends well above each one and into the gaps, so you don't need to aim. Tapping anywhere on the card reveals the answer, and for a fraction of a second afterwards the rating buttons ignore taps, so a reveal can't accidentally grade the card. The same fraction of a second runs the other way too: right after a new card appears, its own "show answer" button ignores taps for a moment, so the tail end of a fast double-tap on the previous card's rating can't flash the next word's answer before you meant to see it.

Made a mistake? The arrow at the top right undoes the last rating, up to forty back.

On a keyboard — a Mac or an iPad with one attached — Space or Enter reveals the answer, and once it's showing, Space or Enter again rates it Good, the same double duty Space has in Anki. 1, 2 and 3 rate Again, Good and Easy directly — I, O and P do the same thing, for whichever hand you'd rather use — and Z undoes.

7. Backing up — read this one

Everything lives on the device you are using. There is no account and no server. Losing it means losing every interval the app has learned about you, which is worth much more than the word list itself.

Three things protect you, in increasing order of reliability:

  • It saves after every single rating, into two separate places in the browser, and checks the save actually landed. If it ever fails you'll see a red warning on the front screen — act on it.
  • Backup files are saved by hand, never automatically. Safari silently blocks a download no tap asked for, so an automatic file backup could never be trusted — there isn't one. Use Export a backup file in settings before anything risky; and when the gist is off or has gone quiet and fifty ratings have built up, a Back up now button appears in settings too. On iPhone the file lands in Files → Downloads.
  • A private GitHub gist is the primary backup. Fill in a gist id and a token in settings and every save is pushed there a few seconds later. The front screen shows how long ago that last worked, and turns amber then red as it ages. The token is never written into the gist or into a backup file. One gist serves both apps — Latin keeps its progress in vocabula.json, Greek in vocabula-greek.json.

Using it on more than one device. Give each device its own name first, in This device's name — "iPhone", "iPad", "Mac" — then put the same gist id and token into every device you want kept in step. Each device pushes a few seconds after you stop rating, and pulls by itself when you open the app or return to it from the background, so the iPad picks up what you did on the phone without your having to think about it. Two rules make this safe. It only adopts the gist copy when this device has nothing newer of its own, so an automatic pull can never swallow reviews you have just done. And if both sides have moved on — you studied on the phone and the iPad while one of them was offline — it refuses to guess: it names both copies and asks — "This device (iPad) has 2 reviews the gist does not have yet. The gist's copy was last pushed by iPhone and has 4 reviews this device does not have." OK takes the gist's copy; Cancel changes nothing and asks again next time, so a stray tap or an Escape key on a Mac can never quietly overwrite anything. If you want to keep this device's copy instead, use Push to gist now in settings when you're sure — it and Pull from gist both name whichever copy they're acting on, too. The two histories cannot be merged, so whichever copy you end up keeping, the other side's reviews since the split are lost; the fix is to finish a session on one device before starting on another.

Install it to your Home Screen. Open it in Safari, tap Share, then Add to Home Screen, and use it from that icon rather than from a browser tab. Safari deletes stored data for websites you haven't visited in a week; installed apps are exempt. This matters more than anything else on this page.

A study day begins atThe hour everything scheduled for a day unlocks, together. Before it, a late-night session still counts as the previous day.
AppearanceLight, dark, or follow your phone.
Unlock the reverse direction afterHow long a word must hold in the forward direction before you meet it backwards. 0 removes the gate.

Clearing a word for the dayWhether Good is enough to release a new word, or only Easy is.
Consecutive good days before releaseForces a word to a daily rhythm until you've got it right this many days running. Leave at 0 — it is very expensive and works against the spacing that makes the whole thing effective.
Weekly review dayNudges medium-length intervals onto one weekday, by up to three days either way.
Head start for known wordsHow long before a word you ticked in the audit is first tested.
Introduce unseen before shakyHardest words first, rather than working through in book order.
Michaelmas beginsFeeds the countdown on the front screen. Nothing else uses it.
ThemeSeven palettes, each with its own light and dark variant, chosen separately. Optionally a different one each day, in a shuffled cycle that shows every theme before repeating any.
Layout"Legacy" (the default) is the layout you already know, on any screen, at any size. "New" adds a second layout the app switches to on a screen at least 700px wide, turned sideways — a laptop or an iPad, not a phone in your hand: the study card becomes a two-pane spread, the front screen's sections group into two columns, Settings reads in two columns, and Browse gets a fixed filter panel beside the results. On a phone the two look identical; there's no "always sideways" option, since that would look wrong held upright.
Export / restoreSave a backup file by hand, or load one back in. A file from the other language is refused rather than loaded over the top.
This device's nameA label such as "iPhone" or "Mac", local to this device. Stamped onto anything this device pushes or exports, so a sync conflict on any device names both copies instead of just "this device" and "the gist".
Back up to a GitHub gistThe gist id and token — the primary backup. Pushes after every save. Restoring or pulling never changes the id, token or device name stored on this device, and the token is never included in what gets pushed.
What happens on its ownLive state — last save, last push, what the queue is holding — and a list of every process the app runs without being asked.
Set asideOnce "Again" has landed on a word 5 times in a single study day, a flag appears on the card that you can tap to set it aside by hand; 8 times in a day it happens on its own. Setting a word aside stops it appearing until you bring it back, either from the flag itself or in bulk here — and it stays aside regardless of the day, even once today's count resets.
Erase all progressPushes a backup to the gist first and only wipes the device once that push is confirmed. Needs gist backup set up — without it, this refuses, since there's no reliable way to confirm a local download actually happened.

9. If something looks wrong

English to Latin is empty

That's deliberate. Reverse cards unlock only after the forward card has held for a fortnight. The front screen tells you how many are waiting. Set the unlock threshold to 0 in settings if you'd rather have them all now.

I've suddenly got hundreds due

Either you missed some days, or a big batch you learned together has come due together. Set new words to 0 until it clears. Don't skip the backlog — those are the words at the exact moment they're worth reviewing.

An interval looks far too long

A long gap is only ever given to a word the app has good evidence you know. If it genuinely feels wrong, press a lower grade next time you see it — that's the correction mechanism, and it works immediately. If it feels wrong across the board, raise target retention a little.

I've been away a fortnight

Just start again. Overdue words are handled properly — the app knows how long it's been, and getting one right after a long gap counts for more than getting it right on time.

A word keeps beating me

If a word is beating you badly in one sitting, that's worth listening to — a flag appears on the card once Again has landed on it 5 times in a single study day, which you can tap to set it aside by hand. 8 times in a day it happens on its own — at that point the answer isn't more repetitions, it's learning the word some other way, then bringing it back when you're ready. The front screen's Struggling words section lists every word at 5+ Agains today, ranked worst first, plus everything you've actually set aside (which keeps showing there regardless of the day), so you can see the whole list of what's stuck without waiting to meet each one in a session. A small × below the flag dismisses that warning for now without setting the word aside — for acknowledging a rough answer without committing to pulling the word out of rotation; the automatic set-aside at 8 still happens on its own regardless of whether the warning was dismissed. Once a word is set aside, Drilling your set-aside words above is a way to work on it directly, as often as you like, without it affecting your real scheduling.

The counts changed when I toggled something

Part and group chips filter which words can start. They never hide a word already in the rotation, and they never affect anything you've already rated.

How do I know which version I'm running?

The version sits at the bottom of the front screen. Tap it for the list of what changed. If the front screen has no whole syllabus section, you are on an older file.

Both apps work the same way. Greek has no parts and no audit groups, so those chips don't appear, and it starts entirely from scratch. Your Latin and Greek progress are stored separately and can't affect each other.
