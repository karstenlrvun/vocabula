How this works
Opens the manual, if it is sitting alongside this file.
Pushes a backup to the gist first and only erases once that push is confirmed. Needs gist backup set up above -- without it, there is no reliable way to confirm a backup landed anywhere first.
Grading rule: for a word with several glosses, count it as recalled only once you've produced every sense listed. Otherwise press Again.
Off goes back to exactly how new words were introduced before this existed — the two sliders below have no effect while it's off, but keep whatever they're set to for whenever it's back on. Off by default.
Caps how many brand-new words can be mid-acquisition — not yet graduated out of learning — at once in a single sitting, independent per direction. New words trickle in only as earlier ones graduate, instead of arriving as a cluster that all comes due together and starts looking the same. Never stalls a sitting — only holds back new intros while there's other due or learning work to fall back on.
A ceiling on how far ahead a well-known word can be pushed. The default of 36500 is a hundred years — effectively no ceiling at all.
Everything scheduled for a day becomes available at this hour, together, however late in the previous day you earned it. A late-night session before this hour still counts as the previous day. Changing it never rewrites anything already scheduled — it takes effect from the next time today's current hour comes around, not immediately, so nothing already due (or not) changes because of the switch itself.
Days a forward card must be holding before its reverse card can appear. 0 — the default — removes the gate and opens everything at once. Production is harder than recognition, so a higher number stops you meeting a word backwards before it has settled.
Over 30 seconds, Easy is recorded as Good and Good as Hard. Between 10 and 30, Easy is recorded as Good. Nothing is ever promoted, and a failure is never altered.
Applies while a word is still being drilled, not to mature reviews. Good and Hard bounce it 10 minutes; after enough same-day bounces without an Easy (set below), it clears anyway rather than repeating forever.
Only matters when "Only Easy clears it" is selected above. Lower means a word you keep answering Good (never Easy) gets out of the drill sooner; higher drills it harder before letting it through.
0 switches this off and lets the scheduler choose. Any higher number holds a word at a one-day interval until you have got it right that many days running; one failure restarts the count. Costly — check the front screen load before setting this above 3.
Nudges intervals between 3 and 21 days onto that weekday, by up to three days either way. Longer intervals are left alone so the day cannot fill up without limit.
Days before a known word is first tested. Only applies to words not yet introduced. Longer means they surface as an occasional spot-check rather than a lesson.
Lower parks a struggling word sooner; higher gives it more real, spaced attempts first. Raising this never brings an already-set-aside word back on its own. Lowering it takes effect at once for any new "Again" press, but never reaches back to reinterpret presses already logged earlier today. Whatever it is set to, the study card warns you one press before it happens, and that warning can be overridden for the rest of the day.
